Research date: September 15, 2026. This is a point-in-time research deliverable, not application configuration or verified customer discovery.

This research phase includes nine completed iterative parallel rounds, followed by source and commercial cross-checks and a financial-protocol comparison using the inspected Orcus and Moros systems as depth benchmarks, not products to copy. The user rejected the private release-validation, bug-bounty, privacy-wrapped lending, narrow credit-market, founder capital-formation, infrastructure-only, and proof-of-commerce directions. Section 19 is historical research, not the current recommendation. A later cross-ecosystem consumer-product round selected [CareBond, a funded product-care and repair-clearing protocol](./arbitrum-consumer-protocol-decision-2026-09-16.md). Current research recommendation: validate and build CareBond only if at least two D2C hardware brands will fund a bounded repair reserve and five qualified repair shops will quote jobs without lead fees. The product must combine restricted reserve capital, transferable product rights, competitive repair procurement, signed handoff, outcome settlement, disputes, and recall state. Do not start implementation from historical FeeForward, bounty-platform, Blindbook, capacity-market, generic Omnipair-port, Yumi-like credit, Kormos-like vault, ODL-like recovery, or Senthos-like prime-broker recommendations. This is a conditional startup thesis, not demonstrated product-market fit.

The superseded capacity-market comparison found LIKWID already implements native spot plus withdrawable credit, with mainnet listings on Ethereum, Base and BNB; Timeswap already serves oracleless Arbitrum credit; Fluid/EulerSwap reuse liquidity across trading and borrowing. Reserve floors, queues and insurance alone are insufficient originality. The narrower participant right was funded, time-bounded cash availability under explicit collateral conditions, but it did not survive the final company-level comparison. [Historical product, competitive comparison, lifecycle, revenue scenarios and kill criteria](../specs/capacity-native-credit-product-thesis.md)

Current priority: obtain two written brand pilot intents, one real funded 20-product reserve, and five repair-shop commitments before generalizing CareBond. Candidate F remains rejected. Category security spending did not establish a compelling customer switching advantage over mature incumbents or an Arbitrum-specific product need. Do not implement it or describe it as the selected startup.

The most specific surviving rate-market hypothesis is prepaid, bounded protection against accumulated borrowing costs on an existing lending position. It has genuine protocol substance, but fresh evidence weakens the startup case: much of its plumbing already exists in Kairos, the initial Arbitrum market is smaller than assumed, and exact-expiry settlement and manipulation economics remain unresolved. It is a candidate for priced validation, not a selected or funding-ready company.

A broader round found stronger documented business need in purpose-bound solver financing. A focused follow-up found that Sprinter already offers the core job on Arbitrum, including an API for independent solvers. Generic solver fill financing is therefore not selected. A neutral overflow-capital market remains an unvalidated market-structure hypothesis, not a new credit primitive. Candidate D below records the competitor, sampled economics and enforcement gaps.

Binding GM/GLV exit-capacity markets are an alternative, not a demonstrated improvement. GMX documents the liquidity constraint, but recurring paid demand for forward capacity has not been established. Generic fixed-credit, rate-swap, liquidation-auction, RFQ, or yield-tokenization ports are not original product recommendations.

Focused rounds identified Aave DAO's GHO operations as an issuer prospect with documented liquidity costs and Arbitrum exposure, then found no current unserved One-specific obligation or open procurement for our proposed right. Forgd already offers free competitive procurement and monitoring, Arrakis recommends contractual penalties, and DSLA supplies close historical collateralized-SLA prior art on Arbitrum. Cork overlaps the proposed reserved conversion right. GHO remains a possible discovery contact, not the basis of an original startup recommendation.

The requested bar is a complete startup product backed by enforceable economic rules. A difficult contract, a protocol token, more adapters, or a polished dashboard does not establish that bar. Winning is an outcome to pursue, not a research claim or a guarantee.

Originality does not require a first-ever mathematical primitive or a market with no competitors. Borrowing proven mechanisms is acceptable. Our own product needs a specific customer, a measurable reason to switch, a complete transaction lifecycle and viable contribution economics. Prior art narrows differentiation; it is not an automatic veto. Conversely, an unverified absence on Arbitrum does not establish demand.

## 2. Research method and evidence standard

Research runs in repeated independent rounds with at most three specialist agents alongside the primary researcher. The streams challenge competition, business economics, and mainnet feasibility. Linkup is called through its HTTP API, without installing its SDK. Search summaries supply leads; primary project documentation, operator reports, official event pages, and read-only mainnet/API observations support conclusions.

Evidence labels used here:

- Verified mechanism: described in primary documentation or source. This does not independently prove a safe deployed implementation.
- Operator-reported traction: the project or ecosystem reports volume, users, commitments, or deposits. These are not audited revenue figures.
- Observed snapshot: a timestamped API or read-only mainnet measurement. It is not a permanent market fact.
- Business hypothesis: proposed buyer, price, distribution, or behavior that has not been confirmed with actual counterparties.
- Unverified absence: a bounded search did not find a particular product. This is not proof nobody has built it or an incumbent cannot add it.

No customer interviews, independent counterparty commitments, executable quotes, original paid revenue, audit completion, or deployment of our proposed product have been demonstrated in this research. We have not contacted third parties or moved funds.

- There is no verified universal list of missing Arbitrum primitives.
- A protocol live on Solana but not found on Arbitrum does not prove an Arbitrum buyer exists.
- Existing lending debt, LP principal, transfer volume, or derivatives notional is not our revenue TAM.

- A long architecture can conceal one unvalidated transaction that nobody wants to purchase.

## 7. Candidate A: existing-debt borrowing-cost options

### Buyer job and exact promise

An operator with material, durable Aave debt wants predictable borrowing expenditure but still wants to benefit if rates fall. It does not want to close the underlying position merely to hedge.

Proposed promise: keep the loan where it is; pay an upfront premium; receive an offset if the accepted borrowing-cost benchmark exceeds the purchased budget, up to the disclosed maximum payout.

This is an option on a fixed inception notional, not insurance of whatever changing debt happens to be in the wallet. It does not prevent Aave liquidation, repay principal, guarantee maximum APR, cover every outage, or protect an unlimited tail. Buyer premiums are a real additional cost even if rates fall.

### Surviving differentiation

Kairos already describes hedging an untouched existing loan using native cumulative indexes. IPOR and Boros already hedge rates with bounded collateral outcomes. A properly prefunded pay-fixed swap against a nonnegative borrowing index need not require principal-sized collateral. The cap's surviving difference is one-sided payoff: retain favorable falling rates in return for paying for convexity upfront. [Kairos borrower flow](https://kairosswap.com/fix-rate), [IPOR swaps](https://docs.ipor.io/ipor-derivatives/automated-market-maker/ipor-swaps), [Boros protection mechanics](https://docs.pendle.finance/boros-docs/boros-systems/protective-mechanism)

No verified live product with the entire exact combination was found in the inspected set. That does not establish worldwide novelty. A bilateral-swap incumbent can plausibly offer options later.

### Illustrative economic terms, not market quotes

Let `N` be fixed notional, `K` the purchased annualized budget, `d` the accepted observation duration in days, `L` the maximum payout, and `P` the premium. A simple-interest illustration is:

`cost = N * average_rate * d / 365`

`payout = min(L, max(0, cost - N * K * d / 365))`

Production terms would use the selected compounding/index convention, not this spreadsheet approximation.

For `N = $1m`, `d = 30`, `K = 6%`, `L = $10,000`, and hypothetical `P = $500`:

| Average simple APR | Underlying borrowing cost | Option payout | Cost after payout plus premium |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 3.51% | $2,884.93 | $0 | $3,384.93 |
| 10% | $8,219.18 | $3,287.67 | $5,431.51 |
| 14% | $11,506.85 | $6,575.34 | $5,431.51 |

The current native-USDC static interest curve observed through Aave's official API has 0% base, 4% slope1, 10% slope2, and 90% optimal utilization: a 14% static maximum APR. Governance can change it. Thus a 20% example is a governance-change counterfactual, not a utilization-only outcome under this observed curve. The $10k payout ceiling would not be exhausted by a 14% constant simple-rate month in this example.

Five percentage points APR on $1m for thirty days costs $4,109.59, not $410.96. The smaller figure is for $100k. Keep principal, time, APR/APY, and compounding units explicit.

### Revenue and market ceiling

Hypothesis: platform charges a disclosed percentage of paid premium. Buyer pays protection premium; writer receives premium less the fee and bears payouts. Escrow collateral, derivative notional, underlying debt, and writer premium are not platform revenue.

At a hypothetical 5% platform fee on the $500 example premium, the platform earns $25 per $1m of thirty-day coverage. Writer receives $475 while reserving $10k. Its expected result is:

`475 - expected_payout - capital_opportunity_cost - execution_and_servicing_cost`

At a hypothetical 5% alternative annual return, thirty-day opportunity cost of $10k is $41.10. We do not have an empirically justified loss probability, quoted premium, or underwriting margin.

### Mainnet feasibility blockers

- Exact expiry: current Aave normalized debt is readable; authentic historical `I(T)` after later reserve updates has not been demonstrated. First-post-expiry observation, interpolation, and authenticated history are different products. Kairos already documents interpolation and failure conventions; those are not our novel solution. [Aave reserve source](https://github.com/aave-dao/aave-v3-origin/blob/main/src/contracts/protocol/libraries/logic/ReserveLogic.sol), [Kairos views](https://docs.kairosswap.com/protocol/views)
- Observation incentives: somebody must submit each needed transaction and pay gas. Permissionless access does not guarantee a timely observation. Buyer and writer may prefer different closing times.
- Manipulation: utilization, supplier withdrawal, sustained borrowing, and recycled interest can influence borrowing growth. Public data is not manipulation-proof. Aggregate synthetic exposure matters more than per-wallet limits. IPOR's documented sustained-index attack is relevant prior art, not proof of a profitable attack on this specific proposed reserve. [IPOR operator incident report](https://blog.ipor.io/ipor-usdt-index-sustained-oracle-attack-and-risk-mitigation-4f3618876a2c)
- Source failure: empty/deprecated reserves, index regression, upgrades, token identity changes, blocked transfers, and persistent read failure need explicit economic outcomes. A silent monotonic clamp changes the benchmark. Collateral accounting cannot force a frozen token to transfer.
- Hedge mismatch: later debt additions underhedge; repayments and liquidation can overhedge. An end-of-term payout does not supply cash during an earlier liquidation.

These are launch gates, not impressive extra modules to add to a pitch.

## 8. Candidate B: binding GM/GLV exit capacity

### Proposed economic object

A holder purchases an enforceable reservation of future USDC exit capacity. A maker commits funded capacity, later taking delivery of GM/GLV shares under disclosed pricing and exercise rules. Cash comes from the maker, not a promise that GMX must redeem on demand.

Prospective payer: a leveraged holder or strategy operator with a dated cash obligation. The actual recurring user loss and willingness to pay have not been measured.

GMX documents that withdrawals can be unavailable when available liquidity reaches zero. Dolomite's pause sentinels react to withdrawal-disabled and PnL-factor conditions. An alternative is a fully funded physical put: buyer may deliver a precise quantity of a transferable GM token for reserved strike USDC before expiry. Do not select until two holders show costly actual exit events and two independent makers quote funded terms that cross without subsidy. A best-effort exit router is not the proposed protocol.

It remains a potential research experiment for genuinely stationary LPs, not the selected product. ## 10. Full-protocol acceptance bar for any selected idea

These are product requirements to satisfy before committing to implementation, not a contract architecture or permission to deploy.

| Economic responsibility | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Market terms | Identify exact assets/reference, units, economic term, fee, rights, maximum loss, payout and failure conventions. Never identify reserves by symbol alone |
| Independent supply | An unrelated provider can quote and commit funded risk/capacity without a team-only role |
| Acceptance | Both parties accept the same terms; funding cannot be reused to promise mutually incompatible obligations |
| Position ownership | Claim holder and delegated actions are explicit. Contract integrations can own positions without ambiguous withdrawal authority |
| Active obligations | Exposure cannot silently increase or terms change after acceptance. Source/identity changes have predefined consequences |
| Ending a position | Identify caller, incentive, gas, valid observations, payout, unused-capital release, and deterministic longstop/failure outcome |
| Shutdown and dependencies | Team shutdown stops neither authorized claims nor the specified exits. Market shutdown can stop new risk without rewriting existing obligations |
| Integrations | Events and realized-claim semantics support a real independent consumer. Transferability is not promised liquidity, and accrued payout is not automatically a fair sale value |
| Economic safety | Test adverse selection, concentration, synthetic exposure, correlated exercises, attack incentives and capital cost, not only arithmetic solvency |
| Operations | Separate the risks of contracts, index sources, token controls, chain availability, keeper service, and quoted liquidity |

The website is a product surface, not the arbiter of obligations. A surviving market also needs writers, buyers, transactions, and readable dependencies. 'Works without the team' does not mean 'nobody has to transact.' Governance cannot retroactively change accepted strike, settlement, fees, claim ownership, or reserved capital.

Do not add continuously redeemable underwriting vaults until conservative valuation and withdrawal semantics are justified. Do not add a token to subsidize missing buyers. Do not promise automatic rollovers without authorization and solvent new counterparties.

## 11. Full-product form and business operation

For the financial-market candidates, choose a desktop-first responsive web app, with mobile monitoring and a usable claim/exit flow. Professional comparison, quoting, funded underwriting and settlement are more naturally desktop jobs. A native Android app has no established buyer advantage and introduces platform/distribution work. Reconsider mobile-first only if a selected consumer job actually needs it.

A complete selected product would include:

- Buyer workflow: inspect existing exposure, compare actual alternatives, request/compare quotes, understand premium and uncovered risk, accept funded terms, monitor, settle and claim.
- Provider workflow: reserve or release capital, quote exposure, see committed loss and source concentration, track terms, settle and recover unused funds.
- Lifecycle UX: exact fees, approval/signing status, failed transactions, unavailable quotes, source changes, paused new trading, claim ownership, payout status, and deterministic terminal outcomes.
- Operational system: reliable event indexing, source-health monitoring, explicit keeper responsibilities, disclosed downtime/failure behavior and public direct-contract instructions.
- Integration product: documented economic semantics and an API/SDK only after an actual independent consumer needs them. A speculative integration logo is not distribution.
- Business records: paid premiums, actual company fees, writer capital committed, payouts, contribution margin, independently matched trades and repeats tracked separately.

This is a full narrow market operation. It should not become an unfocused exchange, AMM, lending protocol, stablecoin, AI agent, and mobile neobank simultaneously.

## 12. Commercial decision gate before architecture

The four commercial questions remain hypotheses: who has the problem, what workaround costs them, what they will pay, and how we reach them repeatedly. Desk research cannot substitute for priced buyer preference.

For borrowing-cost protection:

1. Ten qualified operators see realistic premium, payout, endpoint/failure convention and existing alternatives.
2. At least three unrelated borrowers identify actual material thirty-to-ninety-day exposure and a maximum acceptable premium.
3. Two unrelated writers quote maximum-payout-funded terms after expected losses, capital cost, source risk and company fee.
4. At least two intended trades cross buyer and writer terms without founding-team subsidy. Related parties and incentives are labeled separately.
5. Buyers affirm they prefer it to debt switching, repayment, fixed borrowing or a prefunded swap, while accepting loan-liquidation and fixed-notional mismatch.
6. Independent adversarial review resolves the benchmark convention and aggregate exposure limits before external-funds mainnet launch.
7. An unsubsidized repeat establishes more than novelty-driven first use.
8. Actual company fees cover the expected partner share, keeper/RPC, verification, servicing and customer-acquisition costs, with a credible repeat distribution path. Two trades and one repeat validate a narrow transaction, not startup sustainability or venture scale.

For exit capacity, replace the benchmark test with actual dated exit-loss evidence, delivery rights, inventory valuation/hedging and binding capital availability.

Reject the tested segment, terms or product version if no unsubsidized terms cross, if users need a different protection event, or if the acceptable premium cannot support writers and a business. A short discovery sample cannot prove universal absence of demand. More chains, modules, or a lower-fidelity explanation are not remedies.

### Revenue model and turnover trap

Solver pays financing charges; capital providers receive compensation; company retains a disclosed agreed spread/share. Bridge volume, financed principal, reimbursement and all financier interest are not company revenue.

Hypothetical example: $5m average deployed financing at 10% gross annual charge and 20% company share produces $100k annual gross company fees before service costs and losses. At exactly 1.5-hour average duration and full recycling, that average balance requires about $29.2b annual financed turnover. Actual reimbursement may be longer, demand episodic and capital idle. Available pool capital is not average borrowed capital. High turnover by itself is not proof of revenue.

An alternative hypothetical 0.25-bp per-fill charge with a 20% company share requires $20b annual financed turnover for $100k gross. At 1.5-hour duration, financier receipts correspond to about 11.68% of average deployed capital before costs. At 50% committed-capital utilization, that falls to 5.84%; against a hypothetical 5% outside return only 0.84 percentage points remain before losses and overhead. The median ticket below would produce less than one cent company commission, so verified minimum tickets/batching and service cost matter.

### Candidate E: issuer-funded executable-liquidity contracts

This is a different payer and obligation from Candidate B. An issuer purchases a specified term of executable liquidity service from independent professional providers, rather than one holder reserving a future exit. Potential obligations cover accepted assets, directional capacity, reference-price bounds, replenishment, withdrawals, measurement and funded breach compensation. The company operates the service-contract market and collection, not a new AMM or an unfunded best-effort routing promise.

Arrakis's December 2025 Morpho case study reports self-managed liquidity drifting out of range and $5k sell impact decreasing from 0.45% to 0.16% after managed deployment. That comparison changed dates, pool version, volatility and external LP composition; it is not controlled evidence management alone caused the difference. It demonstrates a named issuer's operational liquidity job on Ethereum, not a paying Arbitrum customer for our stronger rights. [Named issuer case](https://arrakis.finance/blog/morpho-case-study)

Critical competition: Arrakis's July 2026 playbook explicitly offers Liquidity-as-a-Service with inventory Arrakis supplies, alongside managing issuer inventory. Native and Sprinter also provide issuer-facing liquidity. 'No issuer capital lock' and 'yield-asset redemption liquidity' are not sufficient differentiation. The inspected articles do not establish or disprove the exact proposed independent-provider breach-right market. Its contractual availability, price and switching value must be compared with actual incumbent terms. [Current Arrakis liquidity service](https://arrakis.finance/blog/looping-playbook), [commercial terms](https://arrakis.finance/terms-of-service)

Arrakis's current public terms, section 4.4, permit protective liquidity/service suspensions for specified security, oracle, chain and other risks without constituting an SLA breach. Section 11.3 disclaims minimum uptime. Individual agreements may change the applicable service arrangement. This establishes that the public baseline is not unlimited execution insurance, not that issuers want to buy it or that every private agreement lacks enforceable service rights. Do not convert standard risk exclusions into proof of an underserved market. [Current public service terms](https://arrakis.finance/terms-of-service)

Full economic lifecycle: issuer commits service budget; providers offer identical bounded terms; accepted capacity and loss-bearing security cannot be withdrawn/reused incompatibly; valid service is measured; replenishment, payment and authorized early termination follow those terms; a disputed breach uses accepted evidence and challenge rules; claims, unused budget, collateral and late payments have finite terminal rights without team discretion. A responsive desktop issuer/provider console plus servicing/integration APIs fits this professional job better than native Android. None of those behaviors has been implemented or commercially validated here.

Hypothetical pricing: five issuers spending $100k/year each and a 20% company share produce $100k gross company revenue; providers receive $400k, not additional company income. For one illustrative one-year $100k contract, $80k provider receipts against $1m average total capital committed throughout that year and a 5% outside annual return leave $30k before inventory losses, hedging, servicing and execution. Include additional bond and idle inventory in total committed capital. Extra swap income, price losses, time/capital utilization and collateral requirements change that result. No named issuer has accepted this budget or maker supplied this quote.

Fatal risks: 'liquidity continuously available' needs an honest measurable definition; public state observations are not proof availability throughout an interval. Wash trades, self-depletion, same-block price manipulation and adversarial breach claims can make the obligation uneconomic. A small bond makes breach attractive during stress; a large bond consumes provider returns. Compensation does not manufacture the missing exit, and an unseizable external balance is not committed capacity.

Initial fifth-pass assessment: strongest additional professional commercial-discovery lead, not a selected startup. Advance only if an actual Arbitrum issuer documents a costly failure its present provider cannot contractually cover, pays for the stronger accepted rights, and independent providers quote profitable funded terms. Without that distinction this is an Arrakis/Royco service overlay, exactly the patch product the user rejects. The following rounds materially weaken this assessment.

### Sixth-pass refinement: issuer budgets, exact rights and stronger disproof

The purchased obligation must be chosen before product design. Three superficially similar descriptions imply different businesses:

| Purchased obligation | What the issuer actually receives | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Performance-priced market making | Providers earn payments for measured liquidity service | An executable trade or compensation after failure |
| Funded breach compensation | A bounded financial claim after an accepted service failure | Liquidity still being available when the issuer needs it |
| Reserved execution capacity | A right to execute specified trades under accepted quantity, time and price terms | Unlimited token-price support or unconditional underlying redemption |

Do not market all three as guaranteed liquidity. Combining them also requires pricing each obligation, not hiding an unbounded promise inside a service retainer.

### Candidate F's buyer, protocol and product shape

Buyer: a protocol security lead preparing a vault, strategy or accounting-contract release or upgrade. Supplier: independent authorized researchers, not necessarily independent capital writers. Job: pay for a forbidden outcome against this exact release, receive the reproducible witness privately, and commission a separate check of the fix.

There is specific release spending, not just an aggregate security budget. Alchemix's completed October 14-November 4, 2025 competition offered $100k USDC across 2,612 lines of release code. Its current leaderboard records valid findings and $12,446 earnings for the top researcher. The operator's quarterly report independently describes the competition within its release process. Start by evaluating outsider privilege takeover and single-transaction cross-account extraction in redemption, repayment or fee accounting. Exclude broad promises about oracle manipulation, multi-block timing, permanent freezes, maximum hypothetical losses and all live Arbitrum contracts. | Lifecycle | Buyer or researcher requirement | Economic rule to validate |
|---|---|---|
| Onboard a release | Pin code, proxy/dependency versions, initial state, allowed actor capabilities, conditions and exceptions | Buyer pays separately for scope work and prefunds capped rewards before participation |
| Research | Independent researchers locally test authorized code and assess open funded conditions | Researcher understands proving costs and first-winner risk; no token emissions manufacture participation |
| Submit | Deliver a proof and the privately usable reproducer bound to it | A proof of existence or an encrypted PDF hash alone cannot earn the usable-delivery claim |
| Settle a qualifying slot | Verify agreed conditions and valid confidential delivery | One terminal first-counterexample reward per funded condition/epoch; earned payment cannot depend on patching or retrospective severity approval |
| Handle remaining time | Resolve ordering, expiry, valid timely submissions and unavailable delivery | No retroactive buyer cancellation of earned rights or indefinite lockup because an operator is offline |
| Retest | Buyer purchases a check of the known witness against the changed release | Separately paid engagement; a failed known witness is not proof that all vulnerabilities were fixed |

Findings outside the funded conditions need an explicit human-review path, not an undisclosed override of already-earned rewards. First-counterexample slots simplify the purchased object; they do not solve arbitrary root-cause duplication or make every further finding worthless. The same witness may satisfy several conditions or release scopes. Before participation, buyers must specify whether such rewards are additive, aggregate-capped or mutually exclusive, with an exact enforceable rule rather than retrospective root-cause judgment. Scope design determines coverage and must be priced honestly.

Recommended initial surface, if this thesis passes: a professional web workspace plus local researcher tooling and integration API. Not native Android, not a consumer wallet, and not a dashboard-only product. The proposed moat is reusable, reliable release scopes, researcher distribution and repeat remediation workflows. It is not 'we used ZK' or 'Arbitrum had no bounty website.' No brand or token is selected.

### Technical depth must be real, not an unsafe mainnet claim

Published ziemen4/zkpoex toy benchmarks using RISC Zero 2.3 report mean proving times of 334.6-892.4 seconds and roughly 296k verification gas on an eight-vCPU, 32-GiB instance.
