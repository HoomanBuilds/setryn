## Comparative recommendation: what to build and why

Build direction: an Arbitrum One confidential collateralized term-credit clearinghouse, working name Blindbook. A crypto-native trading firm's treasury pledges eligible crypto, obtains binding-term USDC financing, services the loan and settles it without exposing a reconstructable healthy individual collateral/debt book. Authorized recovery operators receive scoped information; default may disclose necessary data. The first collateral is WETH; WBTC needs separate admission review. The interface is professional web plus API, not Android. Only Arbitrum One is required.

Three independent follow-up researchers converged on this ranking, for these bounded reasons:

| Rank | Full product | Why retain it | Why it is not automatically the winner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Blindbook confidential term-credit clearinghouse | Closest match to inspected proof-bound private notes and contract-restricted execution; owns funded borrowing and noncooperative recovery | Privacy must survive usable proceeds; current loan witness and creditor authority are new engineering; borrowers/lenders must accept competitive terms |
| 2 | Funded GPU rental-rate-floor venue | Actual financed infrastructure risk and recent funded risk-market startups; full reserves, claims and cash settlement form a complete obligation | Computable already sells the payoff; benchmark licences, operator distribution and underwriting capital are unestablished; rate protection is not revenue insurance |
| 3 | Prepaid variable-borrowing-rate protection | Bounded asymmetric protection can avoid a separate buyer margin position, with native onchain observations | Must beat swaps/term refinancing and resist manufactured utilization claims; premium volume may be too small for a standalone venue |

This preference is team/product fit, not a claim that additional circuits earn funding. Competitor existence defeats an empty-market pitch but does not make a startup impossible. What matters is the financed outcome, enforceable rights, distribution and retained economics.

### The decisive new technical finding

The inspected payment code already binds encrypted output contents to its committed note data. That supports capability fit, not proof that the new loan semantics exist or that the cryptography is audited. [Mysten's implemented auditor ciphertext flow](https://github.com/MystenLabs/confidential-transfers/blob/main/AUDITORS.md) independently illustrates same-amount proof binding; its repository remains explicitly unaudited/WIP and is not a production dependency.

The preferred new hypothesis is a complete loan-scoped recovery snapshot, correctly encrypted to registered operators and bound to every continuing active loan head. Publish retrievable ciphertext, consume only the current head, and keep recovery knowledge separate from ordinary spend authorization. Partial repayment/top-up replace the snapshot; full repayment can privately consume the loan without keeper acknowledgement because no continuing liability remains. Default uses the current witness and existing pledge policy, not a fresh borrower signature. [EIP-712](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-712) does not provide replay protection; [ERC-1271](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1271) signature validity can change with wallet state. Accepted escrow rights therefore must not depend on rechecking borrower consent after disappearance.

This is viable architectural direction, not a security conclusion. The acceptance demonstration requires a stopped borrower, multiple private updates, a secondary executor, actual collateral sale, actual lender USDC, borrower surplus or explicit shortfall, and no double recovery. View-only access or cancellation of a debt record is not that demonstration. A native proof ledger avoids assuming future FHE availability but does not remove gas, proving, key, executor or liquidity risks.

### Revenue without notional inflation

Blindbook earns disclosed origination/servicing fees, not lender interest or collateral TVL. [Term's current benchmark](https://docs.term.finance/protocol/fees-and-penalties/servicing-fee) is 30-50 bps annual servicing: $20M average debt yields only $60K-$100K annual gross. A separate invented 25 bps origination plus 50 bps annual servicing at four charged annual turnovers gives $300K on $20M or $1.5M on $100M, before expenses, and adds about 1.5% annualized fees before lender interest. Those are sensitivity scenarios, not accepted pricing or demand forecasts. If privacy cannot support competitive financing and adequate retained fees, technical depth will not repair the business.

Lender USDC, borrower collateral, startup runway and first-loss money remain distinct. A collateral gap can leave bad debt even with successful seizure. Token issuance, collateral value and a future funding round are not guaranteed liquid loss backing.

GPU-floor fee revenue is likewise only the actual retained premium share or service charge. Natural put writers may be compute-consuming AI labs: [Architect's current explanation](https://architect.co/insights/education/compute-options/) identifies that exposure. They must keep buying matched GPU-hours; merchant operators/lenders writing puts add wrong-way risk. Expected claim costs, cash carry and licensing remain material. Lower floors and put spreads reduce coverage/reserves but cannot silently replace a full-floor promise. An external futures gain is not present reserve cash.

### Full candidate and updated shortlist

The new [GPU rental-rate-floor specification](../specs/gpu-rental-rate-floor-product-candidate.md) owns funded quotes, maximum-liability reserves, transferable complete claims, monthly payouts, disruptions, cancellation and capital exit. It is professional web/API and Arbitrum One-only. It sells rental-index downside protection, not hardware, SSH access or guaranteed operator revenue.

The exact maximum reserve for nonnegative indices is `sum(Q_m * K_m)`. In an illustrative three-month contract with 20,000 hours/month and $6 floors, reserves are $360,000. Rates $6.50/$4/$0 pay $0/$40,000/$120,000. A hypothetical $36,000 premium with 5% retained creates $1,800 platform gross, not $36,000; writers receive $34,200 and lose $125,800 net in that path before costs. A future offshore hedge gain is not available Arbitrum cash. No real premium or risk-capital quote is claimed.

If covered hours are 20,000 but actual utilization is 10,000 at a $6.50 rate, a $6 floor pays zero and actual gross rental revenue is $65,000, not $120,000. This is why the name and customer promise are narrowed. Stopped data must never be treated as a real zero price to force a maximum payout.

| Prepaid borrowing-rate protection | Variable onchain debt creates a measurable rate exposure | Bounded reserved payouts, observations, manipulation limits and close-out | Disclosed premium share or settlement fee | Cheap self-induced rate claims, incumbent fixed-rate alternatives and buyer/writer economics |

### Constructive Blindbook hypothesis, not a selected build

The refined [Blindbook candidate](../specs/blindbook-product-candidate.md) would own an Arbitrum One pledged-collateral and loan ledger. At origination, borrowers consent to condition-bound recovery and designated operators receive necessary position data. Later recovery verifies the current pledge, default condition and allowed distributions without a new borrower signature. Operators cannot arbitrarily withdraw collateral. All accepted loan updates must leave them consistent, decryptable current witnesses; data availability and execution incentives are separate from proof authorization.

Healthy loan amounts require shielded internal collateral, debt, funding, repayment and fee accounting where public movements would identify a position. A public transfer of the draw amount alongside a new commitment defeats that promise despite caller unlinkability. External exits, sparse aggregates and necessary default disclosure remain explicit reconstruction risks. No proof system, circuit implementation or audited mainnet deployment has been selected by this research.

The candidate is a managed confidential term-credit venue for firms already willing to pledge assets into contracts, with professional web/API distribution. Its offer is financing certainty plus continuing healthy-position confidentiality, not secrecy from every authorized party or immunity from default. [August's current terms](https://docs.augustdigital.io/legal/legal-notices/loan-product-terms) already offer no ordinary recall for fixed loans while preserving margin/default and specified termination rights. A contractual confidentiality clause does not establish hidden onchain positions. [Maple/Zodia's September 14 arrangement](https://maple.finance/insights/zodia-custody-opens-interchange-to-maple) is superior for custody-mandated firms, who remain excluded from the initial buyer hypothesis.

[GSR's March 12 executed confidential trade](https://www.gsr.io/insights/gsr-and-zama-complete-landmark-first-confidential-otc-trade-on-ethereum) supplies real use of quantity secrecy between identified counterparties. That supports the information-protection job, not an accepted borrowing premium. Blindbook still needs competitive all-in financing, real lender USDC and bounded loss handling. Origination/servicing fees are startup revenue; lender interest, collateral TVL and gross disbursements are not.

This pass therefore retains Blindbook as a credible full-product research lead alongside prepaid rate protection. It does not select it because other labels were rejected, claim Arbitrum market absence, or require uncommissioned paid outreach before making a recommendation. Its hardest remaining delivery questions are current-witness recovery, practical amount privacy, and a financing offer worth the migration/security cost.

Computable's [current buy product](https://www.getcomputable.com/buy) and [market](https://market.getcomputable.com/) still publish dated H100 inventory; sample calendars and posted quotes are not independently executed trades or guaranteed exit liquidity. Its [August 6 terms](https://www.getcomputable.com/tos) distinguish held, unencumbered reservations from naked selling, require payment and delivery verification, and define operational SSH access as delivery. Marketplace fees are 2% to each maker and taker, distinct from auction pricing. That is a concrete transaction-revenue model around an actual entitlement, not merely a GPU token or scheduler.

Its [hedge product](https://www.getcomputable.com/hedge) separately offers monthly cash-settled GPU-rental-price floors, expressly without changing the existing lease/provider. Current quotes are indicative and counterparties require specified eligibility. Therefore a compute revenue-floor market would have direct commercial prior art, even if Arbitrum settlement and funded payout guarantees could be different. Provider list prices are not a firm's realized utilization, contracted revenue or hardware resale price; basis risk cannot be hidden by naming an index.

These benchmarks justify further comparison of financed infrastructure cashflows and dated rights, not selecting a physical-GPU marketplace or declaring Arbitrum essential to SSH provisioning. A protocol must control assignable inventory or funded financial obligations; hardware attestation alone does not prove future availability or exclusive reservation.

Forward proving-capacity contracts are not a clean novelty claim either. [Boundless's canonical whitepaper, sections 2.4 and 2.4.1](https://read.boundless.network/boundless.pdf), already describes premium-paid future fixed-price requests, breach recourse, permitted resale and baskets of prover commitments. It also examines buyers withholding inputs and provoking wrongful slashing. This pass did not establish those exact long-term agreements as deployed production contracts.

Its [current market source](https://github.com/boundless-xyz/boundless/blob/main/contracts/src/BoundlessMarket.sol) separately defines zero market fees and a 50% slashing burn. An unfulfilled expiry refunds the client's locked price; remaining slashed collateral rewards a late fulfiller or accrues to market treasury. A researcher also checked those two constants against the [officially listed Base market](https://docs.boundless.network/developers/smart-contracts/deployments). A proof-provider payment, refund, burned stake and retained platform fee are different cashflows. [Succinct's current request lifecycle](https://docs.succinct.xyz/docs/provers/building-a-prover/api) similarly distinguishes fulfillment from application execution; missed deadlines are not demonstrated full consequential-loss compensation.

The conditional physical-compute alternative would own dated reservation issuance/assignment, capped funded recovery, aggregate liabilities, delivery acceptance, claims, provider recourse and capital exit. Its proposed buyer is an ML team or compute reseller buying capacity windows, and retained revenue would be reservation/clearing fees, not all underwriting premiums. Its potential difference is independent cash-backed recovery and portable assignment across participating providers. Neither benefit is established merely by a token or attestation. Keep this as a parked comparison, not a proof-SLA clone or selected Arbitrum protocol.

### Compression follow-up: real release from fragmentation, none from exact net margin

Compression can cancel redundant obligations, but a full native clearing lifecycle does not automatically make it a superior standalone startup. [Compression research](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07155) distinguishes retaining trading relationships from replacing counterparties; [Quantile's services](https://www.lseg.com/en/post-trade/solutions/quantile) separately distinguish compression, initial-margin optimisation and capital optimisation.

Take identical binary expiry claims `f`, with one oracle and USDC settlement: A owes B `100f`, B owes C `80f`, and C owes A `60f`. Independently funding each outgoing claim locks 240 USDC. Consented replacement with A owing B `20f` and C `20f` locks only 40, releasing 200. This preserves each participant's terminal cash, including returned collateral, before an agreed fee. The release is existing principal becoming available sooner, not 200 USDC profit.

However, an already exact net-margined house needs only A's 40 USDC from inception. Its reserve rule is `R_i = max_S(max(0, -n_i(S)))`, where `n_i` is participant i's net terminal payout. Preserving every `n_i` preserves every `R_i`: 40 before, 40 after, zero incremental release. Deliberately issuing overcollateralized native obligations would manufacture the inefficiency being sold. Gross notional or contract-count reductions are separate possible operational benefits, not proof of cash savings.

At a hypothetical 5% annual funding cost, freeing 200 USDC for 45 days saves about 1.23 USDC before execution and service fees. Pricing must reflect incremental funding or operating savings, not released principal. No customer tariff or funding rate is established by that illustration.

Current prior art is stronger than a generic clearing diagram suggests. [Opium V2](https://docs.opium.network/for-developers/opium-protocol-v2) already releases initial margin when holders burn matched LONG/SHORT claims. Its [official deployment list](https://docs.opium.network/for-developers/deployment-addresses/opium-protocol-v2) includes Arbitrum One; a researcher checked nonempty Core proxy code, not active liquidity or retained fees. [SYMMIO Options assignment](https://docs.symm.io/contract-documentation/symmio-options-v0.2.1/facets/trade-facet) transfers rights and margin obligations; its inspected options deployment is Polygon, while perps include Arbitrum. [Yellow Clearnet](https://docs.yellow.org/clearnet/learn/contracts/protocol/overview/) publishes Arbitrum custody and threshold settlement. This pass did not verify their exact multilateral compression capabilities, which is not proof those capabilities are absent.

Payoff equality also does not preserve default exposure or grant consent. Settlement currency, oracle observations, rounding, fallback, dispute timing and early rights must match; American or path-dependent claims cannot generally be reduced to terminal-price equality. A new network cannot reassign existing nontransferable positions merely because its solver finds offsets. Standalone compression is therefore not promoted. Keep cancellation/novation as a component only where a separate issuance/settlement offer or genuinely stranded, assignable collateral supplies a compelling purchased benefit.

### Candidate B: deliverable dated FX clearing

Customer hypothesis: a stablecoin-native business with recurring USDC receipts and EUR-denominated supplier/payroll obligations wants a future fixed budget without immediately converting its entire balance or opening a separate offchain hedge workflow.

The proposed product is a professional web app and API on Arbitrum One. The initial asset pair would be native USDC and issuer-approved EURe, not newly minted synthetic currencies. [Monerium's current token page](https://monerium.com/eure/) lists EURe on Arbitrum; that does not establish sufficient present market depth, institutional eligibility or our access to bank redemption. The issuer's [liquidity article](https://help.monerium.com/article/16-liquidity) acknowledges a challenge but was last updated August 25, 2025. Fresh size-specific quotes are still required.

The economic obligation would specify both actual delivery amounts, settlement date, margin policy, amendment charges, default close-out and eligible counterparties. Any margin reduction must be backed by real enforceable collateral/netting rights or committed credit capital. Expected future revenue is not posted collateral. A spot price plus a scheduler, a cash-settled perp or fully prepaid currency storage alone would not deliver the intended working-capital benefit.

[Bound's pricing](https://bound.co/pricing) supplies a concrete paid alternative: published forward hedge fees of 25-75 basis points, with margin/volume tiers, and spot pricing starting at 3 basis points. Bound's charges are not our accepted price or retained clearing revenue. Forward points reflect financing economics, not free profit. [Bound's execution policy](https://bound.co/legal/customer-order-handling-and-best-execution-policy) describes matched-principal offsetting with external liquidity providers.

Competition remains strong: OpenFX and banks already serve corporate FX; Dropset/Mento and spot venues serve onchain exchange; [Handle's contracts](https://docs.handle.fi/protocol-specs/contracts) publish Arbitrum synthetic FX/perp infrastructure. [Pearl OnChainFX](https://pearldigital.com/onchainfx/) advertises deliverable forwards up to T+5 on its proprietary rail. Its broad licensing, scale and safety claims were not independently verified in this round; it still establishes advertised prior art, not an empty category.

[Valorem](https://valorem.xyz/) already advertises Arbitrum One clearing and RFQ derivatives. Its [Clear contracts](https://github.com/valorem-labs-inc/clear) and [trade interfaces](https://github.com/valorem-labs-inc/trade-interfaces) establish generic collateralized dated-settlement prior art; the documented Arbitrum interface pair is WETH/USDC.e, not verification of a corporate EURe service. Our candidate would need superior funded delivery and working-capital terms, not claim RFQ or expiry settlement as its invention.

Hard failure requirements include adverse FX moves, missing margin, delayed receipts, issuer restrictions/depeg, unavailable EUR inventory, inability to hedge offchain and stressed close-out. Locking both legs completely may be safe but commercially inferior to existing low-margin forwards. A compliant launch model and issuer/maker participation are product dependencies, not future paperwork.

Risk margin generally protects replacement exposure, not the entire principal needed to pay a supplier. A defaulted customer's margin cannot be assumed to fund full promised EUR delivery. [Monerium's business terms](https://monerium.com/policies/business-terms-of-service/) impose onboarding and redemption conditions; token delivery is separate from successful bank payout. Commercial-invoice linkage is not a universal derivatives exemption. [UK FCA guidance](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg13/perg13s4) distinguishes qualifying commercial deliverable forwards from difference settlement and trading-venue arrangements. That jurisdiction-specific example requires counsel and should not be generalized to the intended launch model.

Cheapest decisive test: three stablecoin-native businesses provide real dated currency exposures and their current all-in hedge/conversion terms; two eligible makers supply executable competing quotes with capital/margin requirements; one customer conditionally accepts our exact fee and operational flow. Reject if pre-converting or a current provider is cheaper/easier after collateral and counterparty costs. No such validation has occurred.

### Candidate C: committed recovery-capital clearing

Customer hypothesis: an issuer or collateral manager pays for executable purchase capacity when its existing OTC channel cannot resolve a specifically identified asset within an acceptable time or haircut. This is not simply an investor queue shortcut.

Qualified buyers would commit funded capacity for an identified security or assignable claim. The product must own transfer eligibility, commitment limits, triggering authority, settlement, held-inventory accounting, recoveries and capital-provider losses. A recovery NFT is not sufficient evidence of legal title. Product interface: institutional web app and API, not Android-first.

Existing [USD.AI underwriting](https://usd.ai/insights/usdai-underwriting-and-risk-management) already describes security interests, SPVs, cure periods and recovery protection priced at 150 basis points annually. That is concrete evidence that one issuer purchases recovery assurance, but also direct competitive overlap, not willingness to buy from us. [USD.AI's Arbitrum launch](https://usd.ai/insights/usdai-launches-on-arbitrum-autovaults) and [QEV](https://usd.ai/insights/queue-extractable-value) defeat generic RWA-liquidity positioning. QEV prioritizes redemptions; it is not equivalent to selling underlying hardware for every exit.

[Maple's default process](https://downloads.eth.maple.finance/docs/Maple_Loan_Default_%26_Impairment_Procedure.pdf) and [Centrifuge's investor documentation](https://docs.centrifuge.io/user/investor/) already describe significant legal/operational machinery. A shared clearing startup would need superior executable capital access, not another monitoring dashboard or auction adapter.

Cheapest decisive test: one issuer documents an actually transferable mainnet claim, authorized counterparties and three difficult past/current exits; three qualified buyers price it and one commits funded capacity; the issuer conditionally pays for a measurable improvement over its existing channel. Reject if assignment is unavailable, equivalent quotes already exist or sporadic volumes cannot support servicing/security costs. None of these commitments has been obtained.

## Selection standard and remaining research

The validation and product-roast skills influenced the comparison by requiring exact switching terms, separating yield from retained revenue, and recording kill conditions. The building-blocks skill required incumbent/composability checks rather than interpreting search absence as a chain gap.

Before selecting a flagship, compare customer accessibility, economic advantage over the actual alternative, enforceable rights, independent risk capital, retained revenue after operating costs, stress losses and a credible mainnet security path. The commercial continuation below checks current competitor terms and customer evidence. Subsequent research should investigate the remaining buyer/rights/capital gaps or a materially different obligation with stronger evidence, rather than repeat the same funding announcements.

No customer outreach, SDK installation, transaction, deployment, paid commitment or production implementation was performed in this round.

The surviving offer to test is dated EUR delivery against existing Arbitrum USDC cashflows while retaining unused working capital and reconciling into existing finance systems. It must beat the combined conversion, funding, margin, payout and integration cost of current providers. Until real comparative customer quotes exist, FX is watch status, not the next build.

### Distinct new hypothesis: collateralized battery-capacity reservations

An independent fleet operator sells a licensed aggregator a precisely specified power-delivery commitment for a named grid location and interval. The buyer receives capped monetary compensation for independently measured shortfall; the operator earns a reservation premium and delivery payment. This differs from an energy-yield token or a subsidy competition.

The hypothesized complete product owns scoped capacity commitments, funded operator bonds, acceptance, independently attested delivery, shortfall settlement, disputes, expiration and bond release. Proposed interface: professional web app and API. Proposed financial settlement target: Arbitrum One mainnet only. Utility operations and physical data remain external dependencies and do not require application deployments on additional chains.

[Energy Web's legacy flexibility documentation](https://docs.energyweb.org/legacy-documentation/foundational-concepts/scaling-access-to-grid-flexibility), including its AEMO trial, already describes qualification, dispatch and services-market coordination. It establishes prior art, not verification of a current commercial deployment or exact pricing. No empty-category claim is justified. A possible advantage to test is cross-vendor forward reservations with prefunded shortfall compensation, rather than an integrated fleet or coordination service alone.

Arbitrum can enforce agreed stablecoin settlement, not prove physical delivery, utility permissions or the absence of off-protocol double commitments. Missing telemetry is not automatically non-delivery. Compensation is capped by funded bonds and cannot be called guaranteed power supply.

Cheapest falsifier: obtain one anonymized real aggregator procurement contract and interval-meter dataset, then two operators' competing quotes under an agreed bond and shortfall schedule. Reject if current contracts already solve the problem cheaply, the buyer does not value funded compensation, operators reject its capital cost or measurement/dispute authority cannot be agreed. No such access or commitment exists. Parked for now because operational access and Arbitrum-specific advantage are weaker than protocol depth alone suggests.

### Decision after the follow-up

Blindbook remains saved and unselected, with a narrower buyer. Recovery-capital clearing remains conditional on real transfer rights and committed qualified buyers. Dated FX and inventory execution are watch leads. Battery-capacity reservations are a distinct parked hypothesis, not a forced flagship. The six new funded businesses broaden the evidence base but do not establish a stronger earned build choice in this batch.

The validation and roast skills changed candidate status by requiring actual switching evidence, matched economic terms and explicit kill conditions. No implementation was commissioned. Continue research toward a source-backed product choice, but stop treating additional funding examples as validation of any particular idea.

### Conditional candidate: prepaid borrowing-rate protection

This sharpens an existing one-sided-payoff research lead rather than claiming to have invented caps. The offer is a professional web app and API: pay once for a defined amount of cash compensation against a named Arbitrum USDC borrowing-rate exposure, without replacing the loan, providing subsequent buyer margin or liquidating the protection position. Lower borrowing rates still benefit the borrower. The protection payout is capped; the underlying loan remains exposed to collateral losses and liquidation.

**Buyer hypothesis:** the manager of an existing USDC-financed Arbitrum position wants a defined protection term, lower-rate upside and no separate liquidatable hedge. Index Coop is a named workflow lead, not a cap customer: its [current ETH product page](https://www.indexcoop.com/trade/eth) describes Aave V3 leverage products on distinct Ethereum, Arbitrum and Base contracts. Its [dated Arbitrum launch](https://www.indexcoop.com/blog/introducing-arbitrum-leverage-suite) explains variable financing cost. Long directional products are not fixed-yield carry, debt changes with rebalancing, and no current outstanding borrowing balance or premium willingness was independently verified. Expired PT market listings are not evidence of live borrowers.

**Closest alternatives:** Kairos's [current borrower page](https://kairosswap.com/fix-rate) already offers leaving Aave/Morpho loans in place. Its [March 25 funding announcement](https://chainwire.org/2026/03/25/kairos-raises-2-4m-to-bring-the-500t-swap-market-onchain/) reports $2.4M seed, but neither beta notional nor funding proves current Arbitrum liquidity. Its [risk terms](https://kairosswap.com/terms) disclose buyer liquidation and limited counterparty collateral. IPOR's [canonical Arbitrum derivatives registry](https://docs.ipor.io/ipor-derivatives/developers-docs/deployed-contracts/arbitrum.md) lists USDC services, but all six canonical USDC opening functions were paused in the September 15 mainnet check below. That is a dated availability finding, not category novelty. [Boros](https://docs.pendle.finance/boros-docs/Introduction) trades funding rates on margin. Term/Tenor replace financing terms; Pendle/Exponent restructure yield-bearing assets rather than guaranteeing our existing debt expense.

Thus keeping a loan in place, rate trading and using Arbitrum are not differentiators. A narrower possible advantage is one-sided, prepaid, explicitly cash-funded protection with no further buyer liability. Traditional [interest-rate options](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/financial-services/banking-treasury-management/14.7.3.0.0/trotc/interest-rate-options.html) already establish this economic payoff. The proposed business would be dependable underwriting, faithful liability measurement and distribution, not a first-ever primitive.

**Complete economic product, not contract implementation:**

1. Specify venue/index, covered notional convention, term, strike, accrual method, claim dates and maximum cash payout.
2. Competing underwriters quote the upfront premium and reserve USDC for the maximum contracted obligation across all concurrent policies.
3. Buyer pays once; original debt and collateral remain in their existing venue.
4. Agreed observations establish compensation, with explicit governance-change, missing-data and exposure-change treatment.
5. Periodic claims release earned compensation; delaying every payment to maturity can fail the buyer's actual cashflow need.
6. Transfer or close-out requires an actual counterparty or agreed quote, not guaranteed secondary liquidity.
7. Final settlement releases unused capital and extinguishes remaining obligations.

**Revenue:** retained quote/settlement charges or a disclosed fraction of protection premiums. `Gross platform fees = matched premiums * retained take rate + explicit service charges`. Most premium may compensate sellers for losses and capital cost. Protected notional, deposited underwriting capital and investor yield are not platform revenue. No proposed premium, take rate, seller return or profit forecast has been validated.

**Hard constraints:** a composite supply rate, SOFR or another chain's USDC rate cannot be substituted for the buyer's agreed borrowing exposure. A terminal option on average APR differs from protection on every accrual interval. Debt repayment, new borrowing and compounded interest create notional mismatch. Shared-index stress causes correlated claims, so a diversified borrower count does not diversify seller risk. Rate manipulation and venue governance can alter exposure. A capped payout cannot be advertised as an unconditional rate ceiling after the cash limit is exhausted. A collar also creates buyer downside obligations and is excluded from this first hypothesis.

**First falsifiable experiment:** use a real prospective manager's historical Arbitrum debt exposure and desired protection terms to compare firm buyer/underwriter quotes against reducing leverage, Kairos/IPOR-style swaps and Term/Tenor financing, including premium, margin, payout limits, early exit and fees. Success requires a buyer preferring the exact paid offer and an independent seller funding its maximum obligation without token subsidies. No such commitments exist. Public borrowing activity only identifies discovery leads. No outreach, funds or implementation were authorized or performed.

### Cash-funded protection still needs manipulation economics

IPOR's [published sustained index-attack report](https://blog.ipor.io/ipor-usdt-index-sustained-oracle-attack-and-risk-mitigation-4f3618876a2c) describes roughly $40M borrowed across Aave and Compound to move its USDT index, against approximately $97-98M swap notional. It reports a 45.976% index peak and approximately $913 hourly carrying cost. Positions were administratively closed before their original maturity, followed by tighter risk settings. These are the author's dated incident estimates, not current Arbitrum parameters.

The relevant failure test is whether derivative payouts plus other gains can exceed the full sustained manipulation cost, premium, fees and capital cost. Reserving every policy's maximum cash payout protects payment capacity; it does not make the observed rate unmanipulable or protect sellers from economically induced claims. Smoothing or combining indices may increase attack cost while creating basis mismatch against the insured venue. Neither is a free fix. Correlated policies sharing one index also concentrate underwriter exposure.

### Revenue scale sensitivity, explicitly not a price forecast

Illustrative assumptions only: 30 bps premium per 90-day policy, 10% retained platform take and four renewals covering 360 days. They imply 12 bps gross platform fees on average covered notional, before staff, security, distribution, observation, legal and capital costs.

| Assumed coverage | Illustrative annual gross platform fee |
| --- | ---: |
| 20% of the observed $145.41M Aave debt book | $34,898 |
| 100% of that debt book | $174,491 |
| Average covered notional needed for $500,000 gross fees | $416.67M |

The borrower premium would add about 1.2 percentage points of financing expense under these renewal assumptions. No buyer accepted this premium, no seller priced it and no take/renewal/adoption rate is validated. These calculations do not prove failure; they show why a technically complete single-pool cap is not automatically a venture-scale business. Broader distribution or a more valuable paid offer must be evidenced rather than assumed.

### Blindbook: origination secrecy is already occupied

[Arbitrum's September 10, 2025 Fairblock feature](https://blog.arbitrum.io/how-fairblock-is-unlocking-confidential-payments-and-auctions-on-arbitrum/) already describes Fairates fixed-rate lending with protected auction inputs. Its [auction code](https://github.com/Fairblock/fairates/blob/main/contracts/AuctionEngine.sol) and [collateral manager](https://github.com/Fairblock/fairates/blob/main/contracts/CollateralManager.sol) expose allocated loans and subsequent per-address state. Protected bidding is not the same promise as confidential healthy positions throughout a loan's life; current funded mainnet activity was not verified.

[ShadeX's liquidation design](https://docs.shadeprotocol.io/shade-protocol/advanced-topics-apps/shadex-money-market/liquidations) already combines private collateral sales with a higher-threshold public fallback. Its privacy substrate is Secret Network, not an assumed Arbitrum One dependency. Blindbook must specify necessary default disclosure and test whether that disclosure reconstructs unrelated healthy positions. [Zama's June 17 vault engineering note](https://www.zama.org/post/private-deposits-into-public-defi-zamas-first-confidential-vault-design) also identifies single-user batches, subtraction attacks and unshielded exits as amount-leakage risks. No private-loan substrate or recovery implementation is selected. The preserved candidate spec now includes these boundaries.

### Recovery claims versus performing-share exits

USD.AI's [June 4 terms, sections 1.8(d), (f) and (h)](https://docs.usd.ai/terms-of-service/usd.ai-terms-of-service) place defaulted Loan NFTs under administrative-agent control and condition actual loan-right assignments on agent consent and recorded delivery. sUSDai ownership is not direct ownership of a chosen borrower's GPU collateral. The original permissionless recovery-claim premise therefore remains unqualified for this inspected asset, not universally impossible.

The current [verified sUSDai implementation](https://arbitrum.blockscout.com/address/0x88a233b8746da5dfd9c5e82c8a9f9f91b737198b?tab=contract), version 1.12, supports purchasing eligible performing shares and submitting a new irrevocable redemption request with a buyer-controlled controller. Transfers are blacklist-checked. Operator authorization is revocable, and the verified ABI did not establish controller reassignment or cancellation of existing queued requests. Do not market an operator appointment as transfer of an existing queue claim. Source provenance: [official deployments](https://docs.usd.ai/technical-overview/contract-addresses), [Sourcify verification](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/42161/0x88a233b8746da5dfd9c5e82c8a9f9f91b737198b?fields=all).

At approximately 13:55 UTC, the vault physically held about 220.70M USDai, serviced redemption allocation was about 445,914 USDai and pending shares implied approximately 10,770 USDai at the current redemption price. The next epoch timestamp was October 2, 06:00 UTC. This does not establish USDC cash, unrestricted free capital, recovery proceeds or future redemption adequacy, but it does not support a currently overwhelmed queue. The roughly 44.18-bps deposit/redemption price difference is forward pricing, not a verified fixed retained exit fee. [Current redemption documentation](https://docs.usd.ai/depositor/susdai) describes FIFO processing from available assets and QEV as planned, not active.

An independently cash-reserved option to purchase performing shares is a separate, narrower exit-capacity hypothesis. Buyers would accept impairment, pricing and delayed-redemption risk. Without evidence customers pay beforehand for contingent capacity, it looks like a firm-quote exit feature and is not promoted as our deep recovery protocol. [Midas Staked Liquidity](https://docs.midas.app/liquidity-and-composability/midas-staked-liquidity-msl) already documents external USDC reserves, senior issuer liabilities and repayment after asset liquidation; its [current registry](https://docs.midas.app/resources/smart-contracts-registry) did not list Arbitrum deployment. Issuer-integrated financing is a close economic competitor, not proof we can independently acquire recovery rights.

### Mainnet confidentiality and an existing Arbitrum startup benchmark

[Fhenix's current site](https://www.fhenix.io/) and [lending guide, modified August 25, 2026](https://www.fhenix.io/blog/encrypted-lending-ethereum-fully-homomorphic-encryption-private-defi), still describe CoFHE on Ethereum, Arbitrum and Base Sepolia, with mainnet forthcoming. This does not qualify CoFHE as Blindbook's Arbitrum One host, nor rule out every alternative architecture. The candidate remains dependent on a separately demonstrated production confidentiality and independent-recovery design.

Ostium is a useful counterexample to the demand for a completely unprecedented primitive. Its [company announcement](https://www.ostium.com/blog/ostium-labs-raises-24m-co-led-by-general-catalyst-and-jump-crypto) reports $24M new funding on December 3, 2025: $20M Series A plus $4M strategic. March 28, 2026 is a page update, not a second round. The specific switching job is replacing opaque CFD broker execution, pricing and withdrawal discretion with onchain exposure to traditional markets. Reported trading volume is not company revenue.

Its [July 22 current architecture](https://docs.ostium.com/protocol/how-ostium-works) describes Arbitrum USDC settlement and institutional offchain hedging of residual directional flow. [Vault documentation](https://docs.ostium.com/vault/overview) now places affiliate/partner buffer capital before senior OLP in the loss order; OLP also supplies intraday cash while offchain gains are replenished during daily settlement. Net-flat accounting is not proof an external hedge or replenishment always succeeds. This is substantive counterparty, buffer and working-capital design, not only a trading interface.

Its [fee page](https://docs.ostium.com/traders/reference/fees) describes 3-5 bps opening charges, a tunable 30% allocation of opening fees to OLP and the remainder funding operations/development. Oracle charges cover infrastructure; rollover and liquidation flows differ. [Withdrawals](https://docs.ostium.com/vault/getting-started/withdraw) use request, settlement, then user redemption, typically 2-3 days, rather than unconditional immediate LP exit. These are published mechanisms, not verified collections or audited current economic performance. The lesson is a purchased workflow with accountable capital and distribution. Copying another traditional-asset perpetual exchange would not meet our differentiation requirement.

### Distinct discovery offers, still watch-level

1. **Collateral-backed provider-performance clearing:** sell a precisely bounded receipt/deadline obligation with reserved provider capital and a retained clearing fee. This strengthens an existing deadline-compensation lead, not a newly invented blockspace asset. No sequencer control, objective sub-second failure proof, paying execution buyer or reserve premium was verified. Compare [Gattaca's current secondary Timeboost access](https://gattaca.com/products) and CoW enforcement before promotion.
2. **Purchased execution/inventory-loss capacity:** a separately funded loss obligation is different from issuer-paid market-making software. But [GammaSwap](https://docs.gammaswap.com/) and [Smilee](https://smilee-finance.gitbook.io/arbitrum-docs) already provide Arbitrum LP/divergence risk products. Path-dependent adverse-selection coverage would need its own definition: a beneficiary controlling quotes, rebalances or counterparties may cheaply manufacture apparent loss. An oracle comparison alone does not solve that. No matched procurement premium or independent prefunded seller was found.

Both require a complete economic offer and a measurable superior procurement outcome before they can become full protocol candidates. Neither is promoted merely because a benchmark got funded. Professional web/API and Arbitrum One-only settlement remain the intended interface/chain constraints, not instructions to implement now.

### Decision after this pass

Keep prepaid rate protection and Blindbook under comparison, with stronger explicit failure tests. Downgrade the original permissionless recovery-claim lead and do not quietly replace it with performing-share exit quotes. Provider-performance and inventory-loss offers remain watch-level because delivery control, manufacturable claims and incumbent overlap are unresolved. A gap in current swap availability is not a paid switching reason; confidential auction winners are not confidential production loan books; token possession is not legal recovery authority. No build is selected from these verification and discovery rounds. Continue investigating specific funded business obligations and Arbitrum buyer workflows rather than rationalizing the same offers indefinitely.
